# Foundation Thesis — Monopolises

**Version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 2026-08-27  
**Status:** Research hypothesis — not ratified as a standard

## 1. Working proposition

Modern economic power is not fully described by market share, ownership, or legal monopoly status. In strategic systems, power may also arise when alternatives exist in theory but cannot become viable within the time horizon in which a decision must be made.

Monopolises is testing whether this condition can be described and evidenced as **strategic replaceability**.

> **Power is the time it takes to replace you.**

This sentence is a thesis statement, not a universal empirical law.

## 2. Candidate language under test

### Replacement Horizon
The evidenced time interval required for a viable replacement to become available for a defined function, under stated conditions.

### Replacement Asymmetry
A condition in which two parties, systems, suppliers, technologies, or jurisdictions face materially different evidenced replacement horizons with respect to each other.

### Option Compression
A descriptive research term for a reduction in the set of viable alternatives within a specified decision horizon.

None of these terms is currently a scoring system, legal classification, or claim of monopoly power.

## 3. Boundary

Monopolises must not:

- label an entity a monopoly merely because replacement is difficult;
- infer legal wrongdoing from structural dependence;
- fabricate a replacement time when sources do not support one;
- turn analyst opinion into source-native fact;
- publish pseudo-precision to make uncertain evidence appear exact.

## 4. Evidence doctrine

A public replacement claim must be traceable to sources that support the relevant time, capacity, qualification, substitution, or deployment constraint.

Absence of a documented alternative does not prove that no alternative exists.

Gate 0 exists to determine whether replacement horizons can be built reproducibly enough to justify the category at all.

## 5. Strategic context

The research is motivated by the growing importance of resilience, strategic autonomy, supply-chain bottlenecks, export controls, critical technologies, critical minerals, infrastructure capacity, and geopolitical competition.

Context does not substitute for evidence. The project will only expand where the evidence model survives falsification.
