# MON-G0-C05 — U.S. Natural-Graphite Active-Anode-Material Capacity

**Gate:** MON-G0-RH  
**Case ID:** MON-G0-C05  
**Status:** Evidence extraction complete — provisional case result  
**Evidence rule:** S0 + reproducible S1 only  
**Canonical horizon status:** DOMESTIC PROCESSING CAPABILITY EVIDENCED / STRATEGIC REPLACEMENT NOT EVIDENCED

## 1. Critical function

U.S.-based commercial production of natural-graphite active anode material (AAM) for lithium-ion battery supply chains.

## 2. Current source / system

U.S. government sources identify graphite and battery anode material as strategically important and document continuing import dependence.

The United States had no domestic natural-graphite mine production in 2025 and remained 100% net-import reliant for natural graphite. At the same time, Syrah Vidalia in Louisiana had become the first commercial-scale natural-graphite AAM production facility of its kind in the United States.

This case therefore tests whether domestic **processing capability** can be distinguished from complete **supply replacement**, especially when feedstock, qualification, utilization, and total demand remain external or incomplete.

## 3. Replacement target

A completed strategic replacement would require all of the following to be source-native and evidenced:

1. qualified U.S.-based natural-graphite AAM production exists at commercial scale;
2. actual usable output, not merely nameplate capacity, is sufficient against a defined U.S. requirement;
3. customer qualification does not prevent the capacity from functioning as a viable substitute;
4. the scope of replacement is explicit about whether imported natural-graphite feedstock is allowed.

A facility beginning production does not by itself satisfy this target.

## 4. S0 evidence

### E01 — DOE financing and first-of-kind domestic processing
**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Energy / Loan Programs Office  
**Source:** Syrah Vidalia project record  
**Supported facts:**
- DOE issued a $102.1 million loan in July 2022 for expansion of the Vidalia facility.
- DOE described the project as a U.S.-based graphite AAM processing facility and the first of its kind in the country.
- The financed expansion was intended to strengthen the domestic EV battery supply chain.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Establishes the domestic-processing buildout and public strategic rationale.

### E02 — 11,250 t/year designed commercial-scale capacity
**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Energy  
**Source:** DOE/EA-2181 environmental assessment  
**Supported fact:** The commercial-scale Vidalia facility was designed to produce approximately **11,250 metric tonnes per year of AAM**.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Nameplate/design-capacity anchor; not actual annual output.

### E03 — First AAM production
**Publisher:** Syrah Resources / ASX announcement  
**Date:** 2024-02-09  
**Supported facts:**
- Syrah commenced AAM production at Vidalia's 11.25 ktpa facility.
- Syrah described Vidalia as the first commercial-scale, vertically integrated natural-graphite AAM supplier outside China.
- The facility processes graphite from Syrah's Balama operation in Mozambique.
- Customer supply remained subject to production ramp-up and qualification.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Discrete domestic-processing capability milestone.

### E04 — Qualification-limited 2025 operating state
**Publisher:** Syrah Resources / ASX annual disclosure  
**Period:** 2025  
**Supported facts:**
- The 11.25 ktpa facility was operated only at the level needed for customer qualification and critical product-development requirements.
- **618 tonnes** of AAM were produced in 2025.
- **433 tonnes** were sold and delivered, principally for qualification processes.
- There were **no commercial AAM sales under the major offtake agreements in 2025**.
- Qualification timelines and customer purchasing intent remained material constraints on ramp-up.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Distinguishes operational nameplate capacity from actual qualified/commercially absorbed output.

### E05 — Operational capacity and potential 45 ktpa expansion
**Publisher:** Syrah Resources  
**Source:** Vidalia facility disclosure  
**Supported facts:**
- Vidalia currently has **11.25 ktpa** AAM production capacity.
- Production began in Q1 2024.
- A potential expansion to **45 ktpa** remains under evaluation / development and is not current capacity.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Current installed capacity and future expansion boundary.

### E06 — Continuing U.S. natural-graphite import reliance
**Publisher:** U.S. Geological Survey  
**Source:** Mineral Commodity Summaries 2026 — Graphite (Natural)  
**Supported facts:**
- No natural graphite was mined domestically in the United States in 2025.
- U.S. net import reliance for natural graphite was **100%** in 2025.
- Estimated U.S. natural-graphite apparent consumption was **71,000 tonnes** in 2025.
- China was the largest named import source across the referenced import-source period.
- Battery-anode-material imports remained substantial in 2025.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Establishes that domestic AAM processing did not equal domestic natural-graphite feedstock independence.

### E07 — DOE demand-context statement
**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Energy / Loan Programs Office  
**Supported fact:** DOE stated that, once fully built out, the Syrah project was expected to provide about **25% of U.S. graphite needs in 2030**.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Source-native strategic scale context. This does not establish that current Vidalia output equals 25% of current or future AAM demand.

### E08 — Further-expansion public support
**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Energy  
**Supported fact:** Syrah Technologies received an approximately **$164.5 million** Section 48C tax-credit allocation to support natural-graphite AAM production / potential further expansion at Vidalia.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Evidence that strategic capacity buildout remained ongoing after initial production commenced.

## 5. S1 mechanical observations

The following chronology is reproducible:

- Nov 2016 — commercial-scale Louisiana strategy announced by Syrah.
- Feb 2022 — final investment decision for the initial Vidalia expansion.
- Jul 2022 — DOE loan commitment/closing supports expansion.
- Oct 2023 — unpurified spherical-graphite production began.
- Feb 2024 — AAM production began at the 11.25 ktpa facility.
- 2024–2025 — customer qualification and ramp-up continued.
- 2025 — 618 t produced versus 11,250 t/year installed/nameplate capacity.
- Jan 2025 onward — further 45 ktpa expansion supported but not completed.

A purely mechanical utilization comparison is possible:

`618 / 11,250 ≈ 5.5%`

But this is **not** a replacement score. It is only a comparison of reported annual production with stated annual capacity for 2025.

## 6. Capability versus sufficiency boundary

C05 contains a strong narrow endpoint:

> U.S.-based commercial-scale natural-graphite AAM production capability existed by February 2024.

However, complete strategic replacement is not established because:

- actual 2025 production was far below installed annual capacity;
- major customer qualification remained incomplete / commercially constraining;
- current capacity does not equal a source-native total U.S. replacement requirement;
- DOE itself describes a future fully built-out project as supplying only a fraction of projected U.S. graphite need;
- the facility's natural-graphite feedstock originates from Mozambique;
- U.S. natural-graphite mine production remained zero in 2025.

Therefore domestic processing capability and domestic feedstock independence are distinct states.

## 7. Scope boundary: processing versus feedstock

This case exposes a scope problem that must be explicit in any Replacement Horizon protocol.

If the measured object is:

### A. Domestic AAM processing capability
A narrow capability endpoint is evidenced in February 2024.

### B. Domestic AAM capacity sufficient for U.S. battery demand
Completion is not evidenced.

### C. Fully domestic natural-graphite-to-AAM supply
Completion is not evidenced because the relevant feedstock remains imported and U.S. mine production was zero in 2025.

These three targets cannot be silently collapsed into one “graphite replacement” claim.

## 8. Qualification as a replacement constraint

C05 also shows that installed capacity is not automatically a viable alternative.

The 2025 evidence shows:
- the facility existed;
- AAM met stated quality/performance targets;
- material was produced and delivered;
- yet qualification and purchasing conditions still limited commercial ramp-up.

Therefore Monopolises must treat **qualification time** as potentially part of a Replacement Horizon when the alternative cannot perform the required economic function until qualified.

## 9. Provisional result

**Result:** `processing_capability_restored_replacement_open`

**Single canonical strategic Replacement Horizon:** `not_evidenced`

**What is evidenced:** The United States gained commercial-scale natural-graphite AAM processing capability at Vidalia by February 2024.

**What is not evidenced:** A date on which U.S.-based qualified AAM output became sufficient to replace the relevant foreign-dependent battery-anode supply condition, or a date on which the full natural-graphite-to-AAM chain became domestically sourced.

## 10. Gate implication

C05 reinforces four separations:

- `installed_capacity`
- `actual_output`
- `qualified_viable_output`
- `replacement_requirement_satisfied`

It adds a fifth:

- `domestic_processing` ≠ `domestic_feedstock`

A credible Replacement Horizon must define the exact function and supply-chain boundary before starting the clock or declaring completion.
