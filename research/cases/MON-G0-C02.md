# MON-G0-C02 — Domestic HALEU Availability

**Gate:** MON-G0-RH  
**Case ID:** MON-G0-C02  
**Status:** Evidence extraction complete — provisional case result  
**Evidence rule:** S0 + reproducible S1 only  
**Canonical horizon status:** OPEN / NOT EVIDENCED AS COMPLETED

## 1. Critical function

Domestic availability of high-assay low-enriched uranium (HALEU) sufficient to support U.S. civilian advanced-reactor development, demonstration, and deployment without relying on an unavailable or insufficient domestic commercial supply.

## 2. Current source / system

DOE repeatedly documented that HALEU was not available from domestic suppliers at commercial scale and that the lack of a domestic commercial supply chain could delay advanced-reactor deployment.

This case therefore tests a transition from an acknowledged domestic supply gap toward a domestically available, commercially sufficient supply state.

## 3. Replacement target

A domestic HALEU supply state that is both:

1. commercially available from domestic suppliers; and
2. sufficient against a stated, source-native requirement for the relevant civilian advanced-reactor demand horizon.

A production milestone, allocation from government inventories, enrichment-service contract, or pilot-scale capability does not by itself satisfy the replacement target.

## 4. S0 evidence

### E01 — Domestic commercial supply gap
**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Energy / Office of Nuclear Energy  
**Source:** HALEU Availability Program / HALEU FAQ / allocation materials  
**Supported facts:**
- DOE states that HALEU is not currently available from domestic suppliers in sufficient commercial form / scale.
- Supply gaps could delay deployment of advanced reactors.
- DOE created the HALEU Availability Program to secure domestic supply for civilian research, development, demonstration, and commercial use.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Establishes the replacement problem.

### E02 — Program start
**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Energy  
**Supported fact:** DOE created the HALEU Availability Program in **2020**.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Candidate policy/program anchor; not automatically the unique start of a Replacement Horizon.

### E03 — Demonstration cascade construction
**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Energy  
**Supported fact:** DOE contracted with American Centrifuge Operating in **2019** to construct a cascade of 16 advanced centrifuges in Piketon, Ohio to demonstrate HALEU production.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Candidate technical-development anchor.

### E04 — Follow-on production contract
**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Energy  
**Supported fact:** In **November 2022**, American Centrifuge Operating received a follow-on contract to start up the cascade and begin HALEU production for DOE use.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Candidate operational-production anchor.

### E05 — First domestic production
**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Energy / Office of Nuclear Energy  
**Source:** *Centrus Produces Nation's First Amounts of HALEU*  
**Date:** 2023-11-07  
**Supported facts:**
- Centrus produced the nation's first 20 kilograms of HALEU.
- DOE described this as a key demonstration-project milestone.
- The plant was the only U.S. facility licensed to enrich up to 19.75% at that time.
- DOE and Centrus described the milestone as groundwork for scaling production, not as completion of commercial-scale replacement.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** First-production milestone.

### E06 — 900 kg production milestone
**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Energy / Office of Nuclear Energy  
**Source:** *Centrus Reaches 900 Kilogram Mark for HALEU Production*  
**Date:** 2025-06-25  
**Supported facts:**
- Centrus produced 900 kilograms of HALEU.
- DOE described the material as supporting near-term needs and fuel qualification/testing.
- DOE extended the contract for another 900 kilograms over the following year.
- DOE explicitly stated at that date: **there was no commercial source of HALEU in the United States**.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Demonstrates material production while simultaneously establishing that commercial replacement had not yet been achieved.

### E07 — Quantified demand context
**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Energy  
**Source:** Final HALEU Environmental Impact Statement, 2024  
**Supported facts:**
- DOE projects more than 40 metric tons of HALEU will be needed by 2030.
- DOE projects commercial demand above 50 MT/year by 2035 and above 500 MT/year by 2050.
- DOE identifies a timing/coordination problem in building domestic commercial HALEU enrichment capability.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Provides source-native demand context against which sub-ton demonstration production must not be mistaken for sufficient replacement.

### E08 — Government-allocation obligation is not commercial replacement
**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Energy  
**Source:** HALEU Allocation Process  
**Supported fact:** DOE materials cite a statutory objective to seek to make 21 MT of HALEU available through staged amounts, including material from DOE inventories / stockpiles and supported production.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Demonstrates near-term availability actions, but the source itself does not establish a domestic commercial-supplier replacement state.

### E09 — 2026 capacity-building task orders
**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Energy  
**Source:** *U.S. Department of Energy Awards $2.7 Billion to Restore American Uranium Enrichment*  
**Date:** 2026-01-05  
**Supported facts:**
- DOE awarded American Centrifuge Operating $900 million and General Matter $900 million to **create domestic HALEU enrichment capacity** over a ten-year task-order framework.
- DOE described the awards as jumpstarting new HALEU supply chains and expanding domestic capability.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Strong evidence that the commercial-capacity buildout remained ongoing in 2026 rather than completed.

### E10 — Current enrichment-services status
**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Energy / Office of Nuclear Energy  
**Source:** HALEU Enrichment Services  
**Supported fact:** DOE describes the United States as currently having **limited commercial supplies of HALEU enrichment services** available to support advanced-reactor deployment and records the January 2026 task orders to expand capacity.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Current-state boundary; does not establish material sufficiency against projected demand.

## 5. S1 mechanical observations

No single completed Replacement Horizon can be calculated because the end condition is not established.

Mechanical milestone chronology:

- 2019 — demonstration-cascade construction contract
- 2020 — HALEU Availability Program established
- Nov 2022 — follow-on cascade startup / production contract
- Nov 2023 — first 20 kg produced
- Jun 2025 — 900 kg production milestone
- Jan 2026 — long-term task orders awarded to create additional domestic HALEU enrichment capacity

These dates are reproducible S0 chronology. They are not converted into a replacement duration.

## 6. Sufficiency boundary

DOE's 2024 demand context is measured in **tens of metric tons per year**, while the demonstrated 2025 production milestone was **0.9 metric tons**.

A simple ratio could be mechanically computed, but it would not prove replacement sufficiency because:
- demand varies by year and reactor deployment schedule;
- HALEU form, enrichment level, deconversion, fabrication, transport, and allocation matter;
- DOE inventory material and commercial production are distinct supply channels;
- a projected demand figure is not itself a formally defined replacement threshold.

Therefore no percentage-of-replacement score is admitted.

## 7. Uncertainties / claim boundary

The start boundary has multiple legitimate anchors (2019 technical build, 2020 program creation, 2022 production startup contract).

More importantly, the endpoint is open:
- production exists;
- government allocation exists;
- commercial-capacity contracts exist;
- but the primary sources do not establish that domestic commercial supply has reached the defined sufficient replacement state.

A future source may close the endpoint. Gate 0 v0.1 must not anticipate it.

## 8. Provisional result

**Result:** `open_replacement_transition`

**Single canonical Replacement Horizon:** `not_evidenced`

**What is evidenced:** The United States moved from a documented domestic commercial HALEU supply gap through pilot/demonstration production and into a continuing commercial-capacity buildout.

**What is not evidenced:** A date on which domestic commercial HALEU supply became sufficient to replace the previously missing supply condition for the relevant advanced-reactor demand horizon.

## 9. Gate implication

C02 demonstrates that Replacement Horizon must support **right-censored / open transitions**.

A credible protocol cannot require every case to terminate in a duration. It must distinguish:

- `completed_replacement`
- `bounded_milestone_sequence`
- `open_replacement_transition`
- `not_evidenced`

without converting progress milestones into false completion.
