# MON-G0-C03 — U.S. Rare-Earth Magnet Manufacturing Capacity

**Gate:** MON-G0-RH  
**Case ID:** MON-G0-C03  
**Status:** Evidence extraction complete — provisional case result  
**Evidence rule:** S0 + reproducible S1 only  
**Canonical horizon status:** OPEN / NOT EVIDENCED AS COMPLETED

## 1. Critical function

Domestic production of strategically important neodymium-iron-boron (NdFeB) rare-earth permanent magnets at commercial scale.

## 2. Current source / system

U.S. government sources document substantial foreign dependence across the rare-earth supply chain and identify domestic magnet manufacturing as a strategic industrial-base requirement.

The Department of Defense has explicitly pursued a domestic “mine-to-magnet” supply chain. In 2024 it stated a goal of a sustainable domestic chain capable of supporting all U.S. defense requirements by 2027.

This case tests whether public evidence shows when domestic rare-earth magnet production became sufficient to replace the prior foreign-dependent condition.

## 3. Replacement target

A domestic production state in which:

1. qualified NdFeB magnet manufacturing exists at commercial scale;
2. the relevant annual capacity is source-native and operational, not merely announced;
3. sufficiency against a defined U.S. requirement is established by primary evidence.

Planned capacity, commissioning start, trial production, commercial-equipment production, or a policy goal does not by itself satisfy the replacement target.

## 4. S0 evidence

### E01 — Defense requirement and domestic-chain goal
**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Defense  
**Source:** *DOD Looks to Establish 'Mine-to-Magnet' Supply Chain for Rare Earth Materials*  
**Date:** 2024-03  
**Supported facts:**
- Rare-earth magnets are required in multiple defense systems.
- DOD described multiple critical nodes from mining through magnet manufacturing.
- DOD stated that domestic and partner demand outpaced production of any single partner nation.
- DOD stated a goal of a sustainable mine-to-magnet supply chain capable of supporting all U.S. defense requirements by 2027.
- DOD described ongoing investments in domestic magnet-production capacity.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Establishes a source-native strategic requirement and a historical target date, not achievement.

### E02 — Import dependence context
**Publisher:** U.S. Government Accountability Office  
**Source:** GAO-24-107176, *Critical Materials: Action Needed to Implement Requirements That Reduce Supply Chain Risks*  
**Date:** 2024  
**Supported facts:**
- The U.S. imported more than 95% of the rare earths it consumed in the referenced period.
- Almost three-quarters of those imports came from China from 2019 through 2022.
- Rare earths and other critical materials may lack equivalent substitutes at the same performance level.
- DOD assessed high potential national-security harm from certain supply-chain disruptions.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Establishes upstream dependency context. It does not quantify permanent-magnet import dependence directly.

### E03 — Independence initial design capacity
**Publisher:** MP Materials / SEC filing  
**Source:** 2024 Form 10-K  
**Supported facts:**
- Independence in Fort Worth was designed to convert NdPr oxide into permanent magnets and precursor products.
- Initial design capacity was approximately 1,000 metric tons of finished rare-earth magnets per year.
- The company commissioned electrowinning capability in December 2024 and added precursor-processing capabilities.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Domestic capacity milestone; not sufficiency.

### E04 — First commercial-equipment magnet production
**Publisher:** MP Materials / SEC filing  
**Source:** 2025 Form 10-K / FY2025 results  
**Date:** 2026-02-26  
**Supported facts:**
- MP Materials began commissioning the remaining commercial-scale magnet-manufacturing equipment at the end of 2025.
- It commenced manufacturing NdFeB permanent magnets on commercial / industrial-scale equipment.
- Magnetics operations had not yet reached their planned expanded capacity.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Establishes that commercial-equipment production had begun, while scale-up remained incomplete.

### E05 — Expanded Independence capacity commitment
**Publisher:** MP Materials / SEC filings  
**Supported fact:** As part of the 2025 public-private partnership, MP Materials committed to expand Independence to a projected **3,000 metric tons of magnets annually**.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Projected domestic-capacity milestone; not current operational capacity.

### E06 — 10X project and total projected U.S. capacity
**Publisher:** MP Materials / SEC filing and filed public announcement  
**Date:** 2025–2026  
**Supported facts:**
- MP Materials committed to build a second domestic magnet-manufacturing facility, 10X.
- 10X is expected to begin commissioning in **2028**.
- Once completed and scaled, 10X is expected to produce approximately **7,000 MT/year**.
- Combined with projected Independence capacity of **3,000 MT/year**, MP Materials estimates total U.S. rare-earth magnet production capacity of **10,000 MT/year** from its facilities.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Future capacity path. The 10,000 MT figure is not a present-state production figure.

### E07 — Current 2026 operating state
**Publisher:** MP Materials / SEC Form 10-Q  
**Period:** quarter ended 2026-06-30  
**Supported facts:**
- Independence was in the final stages of commissioning magnet-manufacturing equipment.
- The company had commenced manufacturing NdFeB magnets on industrial-scale equipment.
- 10X construction had commenced.
- The company continued to describe the 10,000 MT/year state as a future condition after facilities are completed and scaled.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Current-state boundary showing the transition remains open.

### E08 — Wider July 2025 government partnership
**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Defense / Office of Strategic Capital  
**Date:** 2025-08-10  
**Supported facts:**
- DOD described a wider July 2025 agreement with MP Materials intended to strengthen the U.S. rare-earth supply chain.
- The agreement included financing for additional domestic rare-earth processing capabilities.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Confirms ongoing public intervention to build the chain; does not establish completion.

## 5. S1 mechanical observations

The following chronology is mechanically reconstructible:

- Feb 2022 — Independence construction began.
- Dec 2024 — electrowinning capability commissioned.
- 2025 — precursor capabilities added and trial magnet production began.
- End 2025 — commissioning of remaining commercial-scale magnet equipment began; commercial-equipment NdFeB production commenced.
- Jul 2025 — public-private partnership committed expansion of Independence and construction of 10X.
- 2026 Q2 — Independence still in final commissioning stages; 10X construction underway.
- 2028 — 10X commissioning expected to begin.
- Future completed/scaled state — projected 3,000 MT/year Independence + 7,000 MT/year 10X = 10,000 MT/year.

The arithmetic `3,000 + 7,000 = 10,000 MT/year` is S1, but the inputs are projected future capacities and therefore do not establish present replacement.

## 6. Sufficiency boundary

There are at least three distinct quantities in the evidence:

1. actual current production;
2. projected installed / scaled capacity;
3. U.S. defense or total U.S. demand.

The sources do not provide a single contemporaneous, source-native equality showing that current domestic qualified magnet output equals or exceeds the defined replacement requirement.

DOD's 2027 goal is a program objective. It is not evidence that the objective has already been met.

MP Materials' projected 10,000 MT/year is a future company capacity estimate. It is not evidence that 10,000 MT/year is the complete U.S. replacement requirement.

Therefore no “percent independence” or “percent replaced” metric is admitted.

## 7. Uncertainties / claim boundary

This case contains stronger production evidence than C02 because finished-magnet manufacturing has begun on commercial equipment.

However:
- commissioning and scale-up remain ongoing;
- 10X is not yet commissioned;
- the projected 10,000 MT/year state is future;
- the evidence does not establish a single total U.S. requirement against which sufficiency can be mechanically tested;
- upstream heavy-rare-earth processing remains a related dependency dimension and cannot be silently collapsed into magnet-manufacturing capacity.

The existence of domestic magnets therefore proves capability emergence, not completed strategic replacement.

## 8. Provisional result

**Result:** `open_replacement_transition`

**Single canonical Replacement Horizon:** `not_evidenced`

**What is evidenced:** The United States has moved from severe foreign dependence toward real domestic NdFeB manufacturing, including commercial-equipment production at Independence and a funded expansion pathway toward substantially larger domestic capacity.

**What is not evidenced:** A date on which domestic qualified rare-earth magnet production became sufficient to replace the relevant foreign-dependent supply condition for either all U.S. defense demand or total U.S. demand.

## 9. Gate implication

C03 reinforces the distinction between:

- `capability_exists`
- `commercial_production_started`
- `capacity_is_scaling`
- `replacement_requirement_satisfied`

These states are not interchangeable.

For Monopolises, a Replacement Horizon cannot terminate at “first domestic production” when the source-native replacement requirement remains unsatisfied or undefined.

C03 therefore remains an **open replacement transition**, not a completed horizon.
