# MON-G0-C04 — U.S. Leading-Edge Semiconductor Fabrication

**Gate:** MON-G0-RH  
**Case ID:** MON-G0-C04  
**Status:** Evidence extraction complete — provisional case result  
**Evidence rule:** S0 + reproducible S1 only  
**Canonical horizon status:** PARTIAL CAPABILITY RESTORATION EVIDENCED / STRATEGIC REPLACEMENT NOT EVIDENCED

## 1. Critical function

Domestic high-volume manufacturing (HVM) of leading-edge logic semiconductors for U.S. advanced-computing, AI, communications, and other strategic technology demand.

## 2. Current source / system

GAO reports that the United States produced **0% of global leading-edge logic chips at commercial scale in 2022**. Commerce's CHIPS strategy therefore treated domestic leading-edge logic manufacturing as a strategic supply-chain gap.

This case tests two distinct questions that must not be collapsed:

1. When did domestic leading-edge manufacturing capability reappear?
2. When, if ever, did domestic capacity become sufficient to replace the strategic dependence on overseas leading-edge production?

## 3. Replacement target

For this case, the target requires:

1. high-volume U.S. production at a process technology identified by authoritative sources as leading-edge; and
2. evidence that the resulting domestic capacity is sufficient against a defined replacement requirement.

A fab entering HVM can satisfy the **capability** condition without satisfying the **capacity / strategic replacement** condition.

## 4. S0 evidence

### E01 — 2022 baseline: no U.S. commercial-scale leading-edge logic production
**Publisher:** U.S. Government Accountability Office  
**Source:** GAO-26-107882, *Semiconductors: Information on Projects Funded to Strengthen U.S. Supply Chain*  
**Date:** 2026  
**Supported facts:**
- The U.S. produced 0% of global leading-edge logic chips at commercial scale in 2022.
- Commerce estimates CHIPS-funded projects could raise the U.S. share of global leading-edge logic manufacturing to 20% by 2030.
- A new leading-edge logic manufacturing facility in Arizona was certified complete in June 2025.
- A semiconductor manufacturing facility can take roughly 2–5 years to become operational.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Baseline, national capacity objective, and construction-time context.

### E02 — Initial Arizona project announcement
**Publisher:** TSMC  
**Source:** *TSMC Announces Intention to Build and Operate an Advanced Semiconductor Fab in the United States*  
**Date:** 2020-05-15  
**Supported facts:**
- TSMC announced an advanced semiconductor fab in Arizona.
- The project was framed as enabling U.S. customers to fabricate cutting-edge products domestically.
- TSMC described a competitive U.S. leading-edge operation as strategically important.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Candidate project-start anchor; not a unique Replacement Horizon start rule.

### E03 — First-fab leading-edge technology and CHIPS commitment
**Publisher:** U.S. Department of Commerce  
**Source:** Final CHIPS incentives award to TSMC Arizona  
**Date:** 2024-11-15  
**Supported facts:**
- Commerce awarded up to $6.6 billion in direct funding for TSMC Arizona.
- Commerce identified the Arizona fabs as a leading-edge cluster.
- The first fab was producing / preparing to produce 4nm-class leading-edge chips.
- The award supported future N2 / A16 and more advanced U.S. manufacturing.
- At full capacity, the three-fab plan was expected to manufacture tens of millions of leading-edge chips.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Confirms authoritative classification of the Arizona project as leading-edge and shows that the strategic capacity buildout extends beyond Fab 1.

### E04 — First U.S. leading-edge HVM
**Publisher:** TSMC  
**Source:** TSMC Arizona project page; 2024 and 2025 annual reports  
**Supported fact:** TSMC Arizona Fab 1 entered **high-volume production in Q4 2024** using N4 technology, with yields comparable to TSMC's Taiwan fabs.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Discrete domestic leading-edge capability endpoint.

### E05 — Project completion certification
**Publisher:** U.S. Government Accountability Office  
**Source:** GAO-26-107882  
**Supported fact:** Commerce certified one new leading-edge logic facility in Arizona as complete in **June 2025**.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Government program-completion milestone. It is later than the technical HVM milestone and therefore must not silently replace it as the unique endpoint.

### E06 — Global frontier moved to N2
**Publisher:** TSMC  
**Source:** 2025 Annual Report  
**Supported facts:**
- TSMC's N2 technology entered high-volume manufacturing globally in **Q4 2025**.
- N2P and A16 volume production was scheduled for the second half of 2026.
- A14 volume production was scheduled for 2028.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Establishes that the technological frontier continued moving after Arizona Fab 1 reached N4 HVM.

### E07 — Arizona N3 / N2 / A16 timeline
**Publisher:** TSMC  
**Source:** TSMC Arizona current project page; 2025 annual report; 2026 investor disclosures  
**Supported facts:**
- Fab 2 is targeted for N3 HVM in the second half of 2027.
- Fab 3 is planned for N2 and A16 technologies, with volume production targeted by the end of the decade.
- Additional Arizona fabs are planned for N2 and below / more advanced technologies.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Shows that U.S. frontier-capability expansion remains an active transition.

### E08 — Future Arizona share of N2-and-below capacity
**Publisher:** TSMC  
**Source:** Q2 2025 earnings-call disclosure  
**Supported fact:** After planned expansion is completed, TSMC stated that around **30% of its 2nm-and-more-advanced capacity** would be located in Arizona.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Demonstrates material future geographic diversification but also shows that even the planned state is not equivalent to moving all advanced capacity to the United States.

### E09 — Current 2026 frontier
**Publisher:** TSMC  
**Source:** Q2 2026 results / annual-report disclosures  
**Supported facts:**
- 2nm already contributed to TSMC wafer revenue in Q2 2026.
- Arizona Fab 1 remained an N4-class production site while the Arizona N3/N2/A16 expansion was still ahead.

**Evidence class:** S0  
**Use:** Current-state boundary for the moving-frontier problem.

## 5. S1 mechanical observations

The chronology is reproducible:

- May 2020 — Arizona advanced-fab project announced.
- 2022 — U.S. baseline later measured by GAO as 0% global leading-edge logic production at commercial scale.
- Q4 2024 — Arizona Fab 1 entered N4 HVM.
- Jun 2025 — Commerce project-completion certification recorded by GAO.
- Q4 2025 — TSMC global N2 entered HVM.
- H2 2027 — Arizona Fab 2 N3 HVM targeted.
- By end of decade — Arizona Fab 3 N2/A16 HVM targeted.
- Future completed expansion — TSMC expects around 30% of its 2nm-and-more-advanced capacity in Arizona.

These milestones can be ordered mechanically. They do not produce one canonical strategic Replacement Horizon.

## 6. Capability versus replacement boundary

C04 is the first Gate 0 case where a narrow capability endpoint is strongly evidenced:

> Domestic U.S. leading-edge logic HVM existed by Q4 2024 at TSMC Arizona Fab 1.

However, that does **not** prove strategic replacement of overseas dependence.

Primary evidence instead shows:
- Commerce's broader national objective is a **20%** U.S. share of global leading-edge logic manufacturing by 2030, not 100%.
- TSMC's future Arizona plan would place around **30%** of its 2nm-and-more-advanced capacity in Arizona.
- The process frontier moved from N4 to N2 and beyond while Arizona's later fabs were still being built.

Therefore:
- `capability_exists = evidenced`
- `capacity_sufficiency = not evidenced`
- `frontier_parity = not evidenced as continuously maintained`
- `complete strategic replacement = not evidenced`

## 7. Moving-target problem

A semiconductor replacement target cannot be defined only by a node name fixed at project start.

If the target is “domestic N4 HVM,” the target was achieved.

If the target is “domestic access to the current global leading edge,” the target moves as N2, A16, A14, and later technologies enter production.

If the target is “sufficient domestic capacity to remove strategic overseas dependence,” the sources do not establish completion.

Choosing among these three meanings after seeing the evidence would be S2. A future Replacement Horizon protocol must define the target class before measurement.

## 8. Provisional result

**Result:** `capability_restored_capacity_transition_open`

**Single canonical strategic Replacement Horizon:** `not_evidenced`

**What is evidenced:** The United States restored domestic high-volume leading-edge logic manufacturing capability through TSMC Arizona Fab 1 by Q4 2024.

**What is not evidenced:** A date on which U.S. leading-edge logic capacity became sufficient to replace strategic dependence on overseas leading-edge production, or a date from which the United States continuously matched the moving global process frontier.

## 9. Gate implication

C04 forces Monopolises to separate at least three objects:

1. **Capability restoration** — can the function be performed domestically at all?
2. **Capacity replacement** — is enough domestic capacity available for the defined requirement?
3. **Frontier replacement** — does domestic capability remain at the relevant moving technological frontier?

A single “Replacement Horizon” number collapses these into a false precision unless the measured target is specified before extraction.

C04 therefore supports the thesis that time-to-replace can be evidenced, but only when the **replacement object** is rigorously defined.
